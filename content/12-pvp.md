# Shadow & Holy in PvP

## PvP starts with a different objective

PvP success is surviving, denying an enemy action, and converting that time into an objective. Damage matters, but a full health enemy rooted away from your flag carrier may be less urgent than a low-damage player quietly capturing your base. The following tactics are original recommendations based on the confirmed toolkit, not claims that a particular duel matchup or composition has been proven dominant in mature Forever seasons.

Shadow offers pressure, slows, Silence, fear, defensive shielding, dispels, and emergency healing outside form. Holy offers sustained team recovery, mobile instant actions, dispels, fear, and powerful group support. Neither role makes you a highly mobile escape class. Choose terrain and allies before the enemy commits. The difference between a good and bad position is often whether you have two seconds to cast after the first control effect ends.

No public rated arena ladder was established by the reviewed official material. Do not plan around arena ratings, conquest points, arena teams, arena vendors, or borrowed expansion compositions as though they are live Forever systems. Duel practice and small-group skirmish tactics remain useful; they are not evidence that rated arenas exist. Check later official announcements and the live interface for changes. ([Forever PvP and podcast summary](https://www.wofwforever.com/en/guides/wow-forever-pvp-battlegrounds-honor/))

### Shadow’s decision cycle against players

Before contact, check your health, mana, Inner Fire, relevant buffs, and exit route. Identify a nearby ally who can peel or heal. If you see a stealth approach coming, use Perception before the opener rather than after you are already controlled. Enter Shadowform when damage and physical mitigation are appropriate, but keep the cost and delay of leaving it to heal in mind.

Open with pressure that matches the fight. A vulnerable target with no immediate support can receive meaningful DoTs and focused damage. A target about to be controlled by your team should not receive a careless dot. An enemy whose healer is free and comfortably positioned may be less valuable to damage than a more exposed opponent. Apply pressure, then watch reactions: a dispel, defensive cooldown, heal cast, or retreat tells you what action will matter next.

Use Silence to create a purpose. Denying a heal as a teammate commits damage can finish a target. Stopping a crowd-control cast can preserve your own healer. Silencing a caster who is already out of line of sight and unable to act wastes the window. Communicate briefly when possible: “Silencing healer,” “fear ready,” or “need peel” conveys more than a long explanation during combat.

Mind Flay’s slow can keep an enemy in range for your teammate, prevent a carrier from reaching cover, or buy you space. Standing still to channel while two enemies can freely attack you may be a losing trade. Break the channel and move if the position is deteriorating. Damage-over-time effects let some pressure continue while you reposition; exploit that instead of treating every canceled cast as failure.

Offensive dispel can be worth more than another damage global when a removable protective effect is preventing a kill. Defensive dispel can save a teammate from a control chain. Inspect the actual effect and current dispel rules; not every buff is removable, not every debuff is magical, and damage over time can be different schools or categories. Avoid repeatedly paying mana for an impossible removal.

When focused, shift goals. Shield, move toward help, control the most relevant pursuer, and heal when a window opens. If you are at low health, Death’s potential backlash is a serious hazard. Divine Grace is not a self-rescue. Will to Survive and your PvP trinket need an informed plan based on their actual shared cooldown behavior; do not spend both reflexively on an opening effect if a more dangerous follow-up remains.

### Holy PvP: a practical 20/31/0 alternative

This build is designed for battleground group healing, with Spell Warding and cast protection, while retaining Meditation and Soul Warding. It intentionally uses **Holy Binding**, the launch-period passive added when Binding Heal was removed. Holy Binding heals you for half of a Flash Heal you cast on another target; it does not add an active Binding Heal button. ([Blizzard development notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-beta-development-notes-%E2%80%93-updated-november-2/2360696))

Use the same 20-point Discipline distribution shown for the Shadow PvP build. In Holy take: Twilight Focus 3/3; Holy Specialization 2/5; Spell Warding 5/5; Holy Nova 1/1; Blessed Recovery 3/3; Inspiration 1/3; Improved Healing 3/3; Holy Binding 1/1; Holy Reach 1/2; Litany of Light 2/2; Spirit of Redemption 1/1; Spiritual Guidance 4/5; Spiritual Healing 3/3; Prayer of Mending 1/1. The sum is **31**, so the complete build is **20/31/0**. This distribution omits Divine Fury: Heal and Greater Heal retain their ordinary cast time, while Flash Heal is unaffected. The choice favors damage mitigation and PvP resilience over the faster large heals in the PvE Holy build.

For a legal purchase order, start Holy points 1–3 Twilight Focus and 4–5 Holy Specialization; 6–10 Spell Warding; 11 Holy Nova; 12–14 Blessed Recovery; 15 Inspiration; 16–18 Improved Healing; 19 Holy Binding; 20 Holy Reach; 21–22 Litany of Light; 23 Spirit of Redemption; 24–25 Spiritual Guidance; 26–28 Spiritual Healing; 29–30 Spiritual Guidance; 31 Prayer of Mending. Buy the 20 Discipline points in the order given above. Without Legacy, Holy point31 is level40, followed by Discipline through60. This is an entry order or a PvP-focused leveling alternative, not a claim that it is the fastest solo quest build.

Holy’s opening question is “Who will take damage next?” Put Prayer of Mending on a likely damage recipient, then position so that several allies are reachable without putting you in the enemy’s front line. Use Renew when its future ticks will help and the target is likely to remain relevant. Shield can bridge a burst window. Direct healing answers the health deficit that must be filled now. Holy Binding becomes useful when you and another ally are both hurt: a Flash Heal on that ally also restores you.

Do not become a stationary healing turret. A small step behind a wall can deny an enemy cast while preserving line of sight to the ally you are saving. Moving too far around that wall can break your own heal. Reposition in small, deliberate increments and watch both sides. The best line-of-sight position depends on where allies actually move; a perfect hiding place that makes you unable to heal anyone is not a useful position.

Prayer of Mending is not an excuse to refresh it blindly every time the button is ready. Understand where the current effect is, whether it is receiving useful triggers, and whether a new cast is worth replacing or moving the existing one. Likewise, area healing is only efficient when several eligible targets are actually injured and within the spell’s relevant range or party rules. A large green number in an empty area does not help the carrier who ran around a corner.

Cast protection is not interrupt immunity. Twilight Focus and Martyrdom help against pushback, but an opponent can still have a real interrupt or silence available. Against an attentive melee opponent, a short fake cast can bait an interrupt; stop early enough that you actually deny the interrupt target, then use the opening. Practice this sparingly. Repeatedly canceling your own heals while an ally dies is not advanced play.

Choose Mana Burn only when the team is stable, the target is exposed, and the cast will create useful pressure. It is a win-condition tool in some sustained fights, not a mandatory filler while three allies need healing. Offensive dispels follow the same rule: remove the effect that matters, then return to the immediate survival problem. A healer wins by choosing the right action, not by maximizing the number of different spell icons used.

### Matchup principles for both specializations

**Rogue:** expect an opener and a follow-up rather than spending every escape immediately. Keep defensive buffs active before the engagement and use Perception when a likely approach is imminent. After control ends, get toward allies or clear terrain; fear is more useful when it actually separates the rogue from you. DoTs can complicate a reset, but do not assume any single application guarantees permanent detection. Holy should ask for a peel early; Shadow should not cast long channels freely into an available interrupt.

**Warrior:** physical mitigation and timely shielding matter, but standing in melee indefinitely is not the plan. Watch the approach and use distance when possible. A fear can be ineffective or immediately countered depending on the warrior’s tools, so do not make it your only escape. Avoid healing through heavy pressure in the open if a nearby obstacle and teammate can buy a safer cast. If healing is reduced, recognize the debuff rather than assuming your spell is broken.

**Mage:** terrain is central. Deny long free-casting windows, keep your own healer’s control removable when possible, and coordinate Silence with a real damage opportunity. Do not chase around a corner into a position where your allies cannot follow. A shield may buy time but is not a license to ignore incoming burst. Holy should protect critical casts from Counterspell pressure; Shadow can continue some damage while moving, then stop to cast once the position improves.

**Warlock:** identify the pet and the relevant control tools. Do not assume every harmful effect is removable by Priest dispel, and do not waste resources trying to remove curses with a magic dispel. Use Fear Ward deliberately, recognize that it can be removed or consumed, and watch the enemy’s long-fight sustain. Line of sight and help from teammates are often more useful than a raw damage race. Do not sacrifice a safe position to chase the warlock’s pet without a reason.

**Hunter:** separate the hunter’s firing line from the pet’s pressure. Cover can deny ranged attacks while you deal with immediate needs. A pet on you creates pushback and interrupts recovery, so a teammate’s peel can be valuable. Recognize mana-draining poison effects: Priest disease removal does not automatically solve poison. Avoid long straight runs in open ground where the hunter gets uninterrupted shots. Fight from a position that gives you a route to cover.

**Shaman:** shields and buffs can be purged, so a strategy based on one protective effect remaining indefinitely is fragile. Watch interrupt availability and choose short healing windows carefully. Totems deserve target selection based on function: the one disrupting your control or absorbing a crucial spell may matter more than adding another dot to the shaman. Coordinate pressure with allies rather than committing every cooldown into a protected target.

**Paladin:** distinguish a temporary immunity or protection window from a permanent reason to abandon the fight. Stop wasting valuable casts into effects that prevent them from working, then switch to a useful target or prepare the next window. In Holy mirror-style endurance play, position and mana efficiency become important. As Shadow, use dispel where legal and relevant, but do not assume an ordinary dispel removes every major immunity.

**Druid:** forms and mobility can make a simple chase unproductive. Protect the objective, slow when useful, and anticipate the route rather than following every turn. A healer druid may use line of sight and moving heals; coordinate a control window before committing burst. Do not assume a fear, root, or humanoid-specific effect works identically in every form. Read the actual target state.

**Priest:** expect dispels, fear interactions, shielding, and possible Mana Burn. An enemy Fear Ward can change the timing of your fear; an enemy shield can alter an execute estimate. Protect your mana without becoming passive. In team fights, interrupting or controlling the enemy Priest at the right moment may be more valuable than trying to defeat them alone through all their recovery tools.

These are decision principles, not guaranteed winning sequences. Forever revises all nine classes, and old expansion abilities must not be assumed present. Whenever a matchup recommendation depends on an enemy cooldown, identify it in the live game before building your whole plan around it.
