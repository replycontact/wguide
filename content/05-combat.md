# The solo combat playbook

## How to fight while leveling

### Before your first wand and talent point

Begin with one ordinary enemy at a time, ideally at your level or below. Cast your available ranged damage while the enemy approaches, then finish economically. Heal after the kill if that is safe. A small health deficit is less important than having enough mana to recover from the next mistake. Keep Fortitude active when its duration and your available mana justify it, but avoid repeatedly refreshing a long-duration buff merely because you reached a new quest giver.

Arrange a real emergency key before leaving Northshire. It must select yourself deliberately and cast your available heal. An enemy target should not cause a failed panic heal. Put the current damage spell, heal, shield once learned, and Shoot on comfortable keys. You will use these hundreds of times; opening the spellbook in combat is avoidable friction.

Your first wand changes the mana budget dramatically. It supplies repeated damage without a new spell cost, allowing you to stop spending mana while the enemy dies. That is why wand damage, rather than the melee damage printed on your staff, is the early weapon priority. Compare the actual wand DPS line and equip requirement. An expensive staff upgrade that only improves melee damage usually does almost nothing for this routine.

### Wand phase: approximately 5–21 in the default plan

A simple opener is one cast-time damage spell, Pain if the target will live long enough, then Shoot. Mind Blast can become the opener when trained and efficient for your current rank. Use a shield before an enemy reaches you when preventing pushback or absorbing a dangerous opening is worth its mana; do not assume that shielding every harmless mob is always the cheapest way to level. On easy targets, spending less up front and finishing with the wand can produce more continuous progress.

Once Spirit Tap is active, use the kill as a recovery window. Move toward the next safe target while your resource bar improves, loot efficiently, and avoid an unnecessary heal or fresh buff that spends mana simply out of habit. If you genuinely need healing, cast it. The point is to remove waste, not to turn the mana rule into a reason to die. Watch several actual fights: if you consistently finish with excess mana, add damage and speed up; if you need a drink after every target, remove one marginal spell and compare the next few pulls.

A practical decision test for Pain is remaining lifespan. A fresh, sturdy enemy gets value from repeated ticks; a nearly dead target does not. With a second enemy approaching, refreshing Pain on the first may delay the spell that would prevent the second from reaching you. The priority is a stable fight, then full-duration spell efficiency. Do not build a rigid macro that applies every spell to every enemy.

### Mind Flay phase: 22–41

Mind Flay gives you a slow and a channeled damage option. Use it to buy distance, finish a target while it approaches, or spend spare mana when the wand is weak. The talent alone does not make your current wand obsolete. Compare your trained spell rank, wand DPS, health loss, and drinking frequency. A strong wand may remain the sensible finisher through much of this period. ([Wowhead Shadow leveling](https://www.wowhead.com/forever/guide/classes/priest/shadow/dps-leveling-tips); [Icy Veins Shadow leveling](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-forever/shadow-priest-leveling))

Improved Mind Flay rewards completing the channel because later damage instances are stronger. Canceling the last part solely to refresh Pain early loses value. Nevertheless, movement for a lethal ground effect, a necessary heal, or interrupting a crucial enemy cast takes priority over finishing the channel. Learn to distinguish optional clipping from a deliberate survival decision.

After Vampiric Embrace, use it where its healing and kill interaction have time to matter. A trivial target that will die almost immediately may not justify another setup global. A tough target that is already hitting you is a much better candidate. In a group, it can also support Spirit Tap without requiring your own killing blow on the marked target. Do not interpret its party healing as a substitute for an assigned dungeon healer. ([Icy Veins Shadow leveling](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow-forever/shadow-priest-leveling))

When Plague and Contagion are available, choose the infected target intentionally. An enemy expected to die first near other intended combat targets can seed additional damage. An isolated target at the edge of a camp can be safer. Do not infect a critter to manufacture spreads; the official notes explicitly remove that behavior. Also inspect nearby crowd control and neutral enemies before depending on an automatic spread. ([Blizzard development notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-beta-development-notes-%E2%80%93-updated-november-2/2360696))

Silence at 34 is an important improvement in the default build. Save it for a dangerous heal, fear, long damage cast, or spell that will prevent an escape. Using it on the first harmless cast merely because it lights up can leave you without an answer to the dangerous second cast. Against an enemy caster standing among several others, line-of-sight pulling around terrain is often more reliable than trying to damage through the entire camp.

### Shadowform phase: 42–60

Enter Shadowform before starting your normal damage sequence. On a durable single target, use Vampiric Embrace when you need its sustain, apply Pain and Plague when their expected duration justifies them, use Mind Blast, and fill with completed Mind Flays. Adjust the order to the pull: a cast-time opener uses the time before the enemy reaches you, whereas an instant opening tag is better when competition or movement makes a stationary cast impractical. This is a priority system with judgment, not a universal six-button script.

On a short-lived target, reduce setup. One direct spell and a channel can beat the apparent sophistication of applying every debuff. On two safe targets, maintain worthwhile damage-over-time effects and focus your direct damage into the enemy that should die first. Keep a clearly identified escape lane. Three enemies simultaneously attacking you can erode Inner Fire, break shielding, cause repeated pushback, and eliminate the time you expected to spend channeling.

Death is a finisher with a real health cost if your prediction is wrong. Use it when the target is likely to die from that hit and you are not putting yourself in immediate danger if it survives. Shadow resistance, absorbs, damage reduction, healing, and an unexpected higher health pool can all invalidate an optimistic estimate. Your remaining health, incoming enemy damage, and available healing window matter. A low-health caster should not press Death simply because the enemy has crossed an arbitrary percentage line.

For recovery, leave Shadowform intentionally and heal. Then decide whether to continue or reset. Re-entering form spends mana, so repeatedly dropping out for tiny unnecessary heals is inefficient; refusing to leave it while dying is worse. Keep a normal self-heal key and a cancel-form emergency key visibly distinct until the behavior is familiar. The current disease-removal fix means Cure Disease and Abolish Disease should no longer force you out, but always test your actual hotbar behavior after major changes. ([Blizzard development notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-beta-development-notes-%E2%80%93-updated-november-2/2360696))

### Enemy types and bad-pull recovery

For melee enemies, distance is a resource. Start at a useful range, use the slow when it matters, and choose a position where backing away will not enter another pack. For casters, use cover; if they cannot see you they often have to move, letting you fight on safer ground. Interrupt dangerous healing before it reverses your entire mana investment. For runners, position so that their escape path leads into cleared space; a slow or reliable finishing hit can prevent reinforcements.

Fear is strongest when you know where enemies can run. In a cleared field, Psychic Scream can buy a heal, bandage opportunity, or escape. In a densely occupied cave, it can expand the pull beyond your ability to recover. Do not press fear automatically whenever two enemies touch you. First look at nearby packs, patrols, and the exit. If you use it, move immediately and spend the gained time on a specific action.

Against elites, plan mana and terrain rather than relying on the word “Shadow.” A lengthy fight can exhaust your resources before your health fails. Begin with adequate mana, refresh defensive buffs beforehand, and consider a partner. If the quest is orange, the enemy has dangerous mechanics, or the area is crowded, postpone it. A ten-minute death-and-runback cycle can erase the benefit of several supposedly efficient solo kills.

When a pull goes wrong, stop adding damage to new targets. Shield if it provides enough time, use control with awareness of its consequences, and move toward cleared terrain. Decide early whether you are finishing one enemy for Spirit Tap or committing to escape. Alternating indecisively between both plans often spends the mana needed for either. A potion or emergency cooldown is useful before the situation becomes mathematically unrecoverable.
