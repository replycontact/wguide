# Travel, camps & gold

## Camping fits naturally into the route

Forever’s camping system turns the Basic Campfire into a shared preparation site. A camp can support three contributed features with one contribution per player. Resting or crafting there for at least a minute activates the relevant benefits; some substitute for corresponding class buffs rather than stacking freely with them. ([Blizzard camping overview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24301516/))

The introductory **The Great Outdoors** quest appears around level 5 in guide reporting. Take the opportunity to learn the system and Cooking’s Basic Campfire rather than treating every roadside camp as decorative. Profession-made features can make another player’s rest stop useful even when your own profession cannot supply the same object. ([Camping introduction](https://www.method.gg/wow-forever/camping-in-wow-forever-how-it-all-works-all-profession-buffs))

For a Priest, the decision is practical: will this stop give a benefit that changes the next part of the route, and can the rest coincide with drinking, crafting, planning, or grouping? A minute spent gaining a relevant buff while managing inventory can be well used. A detour to collect a redundant effect immediately before a short remaining session may not be.

Do not assume every camp benefit stacks with your own Priest buffs. Compare the actual buff tooltip and resulting character stats. Do not assume a camp feature that grants rested experience is unrestricted repeatable experience farming. Use the object’s stated cap and cooldown. The same principle applies to Legacy perks that extend camping effects: longer duration has value only if the benefit is useful and stays active during the activities you perform.

Camping also provides a low-pressure social opening. A temporary group can finish a difficult objective, share knowledge of a new route, or become a dungeon group. For a fresh healer, reliable people often improve progression more than an additional minor optimization. The account plan should allow room for opportunities that are productive and enjoyable even when they were not on a spreadsheet.



## Gold, respecs, and the level-40 decision

Dual Specialization becomes available at **40** in Blizzard’s class articles. That is a meaningful planning milestone for a player who wants a dedicated solo/Shadow arrangement and a healing arrangement. It does not prove the unlock is free, establish a live purchase price, or imply class-talent resets and Legacy resets are the same transaction. ([Blizzard talent rules](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24310968))

Before buying or resetting, look at the exact trainer options and quoted costs. The explicitly reduced beta price was one silver in relevant testing notes, while older guide reports list different gold values. Neither is sufficient evidence to state an exact launch price here. Our recommendation is therefore a budget action: preserve a reserve, inspect the live trainer at the relevant level, and compare the purchase with the expected cost and inconvenience of repeated changes. ([Blizzard development notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-beta-development-notes-%E2%80%93-updated-november-2/2360696/1))

A good reason to change a build is a real change in role, not the feeling that every newly discovered talent requires an immediate reset. If most sessions remain solo, keep an effective solo arrangement while preparing to heal the content it can reasonably support. If you have a dependable group and spend the majority of time healing, prioritize that role. If the pattern alternates frequently and dual specialization is affordable, the convenience can be substantial.

The same reasoning applies to professions. Do not repeatedly abandon a trained profession just to chase the next shiny Legacy point without valuing lost recipes, leveling costs, and future utility. A permanent alt can preserve that investment and add new class milestones. On the other hand, a profession that no longer serves any intended purpose is not sacred merely because it cost gold in the past. Compare future benefits and replacement costs, not sunk expenditure alone.



## A compact account review after every major session

Record the Priest’s level, current class talent allocation, earned Legacy total, spent Legacy allocation, and the next uncompleted challenge. If a different character earns a point, remember to allocate that newly available point on the Priest too. Shared earnings do not mean the game automatically chooses the most useful perk for each character.

Review three practical questions. First, what slowed the session: mana downtime, deaths, travel, bags, an expensive crafting bottleneck, or group access? Second, which available change addresses that actual bottleneck? Third, does the proposed change help enough to justify its gold, travel, or reset cost? This converts “optimal” from a slogan into a repeatable decision process.

For example, repeated deaths while trying to finish an overambitious quest are unlikely to be fixed by a small crafting perk. Running out of water because every coin went into profession materials is a budget problem, not proof that Priest is unplayable. Spending an entire session waiting for a group when solo quests are available is a route decision. Diagnose the cause before changing the talent build.

The milestones worth celebrating are concrete: the first self-funded training cycle, the first dungeon healed with controlled mana, the first unique crafting threshold, a legal build that reaches its intended key talent, and a second character that benefits from the first character’s work. A fresh account becomes strong through those accumulated improvements. Sixteen Legacy points are a useful target, but the Priest should be enjoyable and functional throughout the journey to them.
