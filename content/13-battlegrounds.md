# Objectives & PvP progression

## Battleground objectives and a first-session plan

**Darkspear Islands is officially described as 15 versus 15, for levels 30–60, with four capture points, a central flag, and a 2,000-point victory condition.** Holding more bases increases point generation; carrying the central flag to a friendly controlled base adds points. Alliance queues and associated quests are available through the described battlemaster access and the Theramore Expeditionary Force presence at Northwatch. Reputation rewards are separate from simply winning a duel. ([Blizzard Darkspear Islands overview](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/24307308/battle-for-the-darkspear-islands-in-world-of-warcraft-forever))

For Shadow, defend or contest a location where your slows, fear, and Silence affect an actual objective. If you guard a base alone, communicate incoming players immediately, with a number and direction if known. Use line of sight and control to delay capture while help arrives. A successful defense may involve surviving and interrupting an interaction rather than killing all attackers. If you leave to chase a nearly dead enemy, make sure someone else is actually covering the point.

For Holy, accompany a meaningful group or the flag carrier. Keep the carrier in healing range without standing directly on top of them and sharing every control effect. When the flag moves, predict the route and move early. A healer who finishes an unnecessary cast in the old fight can become separated from the objective by several seconds. If your group has no controlled base to score at, healing an endless flag brawl is not enough; someone must establish or preserve that base.

For both roles, periodically read the map and score. Four small fights can be less useful than one coordinated rotation that secures a base and escorts the flag. You do not need a formal premade to say “three incoming,” “leaving one defender,” or “carrier coming east.” Keep messages short and factual. Do not blame teammates during a recoverable game; tell them the action that would help now.

On familiar battlegrounds, use their objective rules when they are actually offered by your live queue. In a capture-the-flag match, escort, peel, and intercept along the carrier route rather than farming distant damage. In a resource-node match, maintain defenders and rotate before a point is lost. In a large battlefield, travel with a group toward useful objectives and avoid isolated attrition fights that consume your entire mana bar. This is conditional tactical guidance, not a claim that every historical battleground, bracket, or queue restriction is currently identical to Classic Era.

For your first PvP session, enter with repaired gear, current spells, water, a usable emergency control-removal plan, and realistic expectations. Spend the first match identifying where you die and why. Was it a positioning error, lack of defensive keybinds, being outnumbered, or running out of mana? Change one behavior for the next match. A fresh character’s first gains usually come from reliable actions rather than a minor stat optimization.

Honor, Rank Points, reputation, and Legacy points are different systems. Do not spend or grind one under the assumption that it substitutes for another. Consult the current PvP panel and vendor requirements before saving for an item. Exact costs, weekly limits, and unlock conditions can change; use the systems chapter’s dated official evidence and the live vendor as the purchasing authority. Never promise Rank14 on a fixed personal schedule based on an old Classic honor formula.

### PvP progression: what to earn, what to buy, and when

Open the Character Pane and its PvP tab before committing to a long grind. **Honor is spending money; Rank Points advance access.** Buying a useful item with Honor does not spend your Rank Points. Blizzard describes player kills, battleground participation, and relevant quests as Honor sources; its Rank Point explanation names battlegrounds and relevant PvP quests. Do not assume every world kill pays both in exactly the same way. Holiday battlegrounds provide additional gains. See [Blizzard’s PvP progression explanation](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24303316/how-pvp-progression-in-world-of-warcraft-forever-works).

The rank reward sequence below comes from that official explanation. Titles are Alliance titles because this character is Human. “Unlock” means permission to obtain or purchase the reward under its requirements, not an automatic item deposited into your bags.

Blizzard confirms a weekly rank cap. Published client data describes a **season-wide rising cumulative ceiling**, not “earn this much Honor every week.” The reported maximum ranks for season weeks 1 through 10 are **3, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14**. Corresponding cumulative Rank Point ceilings are **2,700; 5,250; 6,750; 8,400; 10,200; 12,150; 16,600; 19,100; 21,750; 24,750**. These figures are a planning model, explicitly below official confirmation of the general cap. Check the live season and current ceiling before scheduling play. They do not establish an exact calendar date for your character’s Rank14, and starting later in a season is not necessarily a fresh ten-week personal lockout.

The distinction prevents two expensive mistakes. First, the numbers above are not an old Classic weekly Honor bracket schedule: earning 5,250 Honor is not the same as owning 5,250 Rank Points. Second, currency storage and rank access are different limits. Current-build references report an **Honor holding cap of 25,000**, superseding older pages that still show 15,000. Keep that storage figure labelled as published client data and verify your panel. Spending before you hit storage capacity can preserve useful future earnings; buying something unwanted merely to spend is still wasteful.

For this Priest, the first shopping decision is the Rank2 control-removal trinket and its interaction with Human Will to Survive. Inspect both tooltips, eligible control types, and shared cooldown behavior. A trinket is valuable only if it fits the defensive plan, is equipped, and has an accessible key. Do not assume the Human racial makes every PvP trinket redundant, or that two similar buttons can always be chained independently.

Next build a useful blue-quality foundation. Blizzard’s stated epic-upgrade recipe is the **matching blue piece plus an Emboldened Seal**, with rank requirements on seal purchases. Keep the blue item you intend to upgrade. The vendor’s required slot, eligible item, Honor cost, any Marks of Honor requirement, and equip level must all match before purchase. A belt seal does not upgrade your boots, and an item with a similar name may belong to the other specialization’s set.

Current item listings distinguish Alliance Shadow **Lieutenant Commander’s Raiment** progressing to **Field Marshal’s Raiment**, and healing **Lieutenant Commander’s Investiture** progressing to **Field Marshal’s Investiture**. Use the names to find candidates, then read the actual stats. Buy for the role you will play most over the next several sessions. A mixed Shadow/Holy player can eventually maintain both sets; purchasing both simultaneously on a fresh character can delay the functional baseline for either.

Choose upgrades by the problem they solve. A major Stamina increase can be worth more than a small damage improvement if you repeatedly die before acting. A useful healing upgrade matters if you are keeping carriers alive. A cosmetic tabard or mount can be a perfectly valid personal goal, but it competes with combat equipment for spendable resources. Plan one next purchase and one later purchase rather than treating every newly unlocked vendor row as mandatory.

Seasonal tasks provide another route to both currencies. Blizzard says three tasks in a season additionally award a Legacy Point and that these Legacy-linked tasks remain completable in later seasons. Read the actual task before choosing a queue: winning a particular battleground, completing an objective, and earning general Honor are not interchangeable goals. After a session, inspect the task journal and claim any available completion before assuming all progress was automatically credited.

Darkspear reputation is a separate progression bar, with its own vendor and quest structure. Save its Marks when a desired reward or reputation turn-in needs them, and compare reputation gear with your rank purchases. The most efficient first-character plan can mix a useful reputation accessory, ordinary dungeon gear, and a few Honor pieces. You do not need to wait for a complete epic PvP set before learning the battlegrounds, nor should you import an unsupported weekly decay or seasonal-reset rule from another WoW version.



## A mature character’s repeatable weekly practice

Keep one clear objective for each session. A questing session can finish a regional chain and improve a weak item. A dungeon session can practice disciplined target damage while collecting several useful rewards. A PvP session can improve survival after an opener or objective communication. This is more effective than changing talents, gear goals, and keybinds all at once and having no idea what caused the improvement.

Review your deaths before your damage number. Record whether you had an escape or heal available, whether you used Death unsafely, whether you lost line of sight to your healer, and whether you were fighting for a meaningful objective. For Holy, also ask whether a late movement decision forced you into an interrupt or left the carrier out of range. For Shadow, ask whether extra setup globals delayed the action that would actually finish or control the target.

Recheck talent imports after class changes. A build can total 51 and still be stale because a node moved, a prerequisite changed, or an active ability became a passive. The launch-period Binding Heal/Holy Binding discrepancy between earlier calculator captures and official notes demonstrates why the latest official changes matter even when a database shows a “current build” badge. Keep your saved build documented by names and ranks as well as an import string.

The intended destination is a Human Priest who can quest independently, contribute focused Shadow damage, change to a real Holy healing setup when desired, and enter battlegrounds with usable controls and a realistic objective. It does not require inherited account power, a perfect loot ladder, or an invented arena system. Upgrade the part of the character that is actually limiting your next activity, and keep playing the version of Forever currently in front of you.
