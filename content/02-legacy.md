# The account plan

## Where the account’s points come from

The announced categories are summarized below. Each listed completed challenge is worth one point. The catalogue’s category totals are not a schedule promising every point is obtainable immediately on launch day. ([Blizzard Legacy overview](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/24307383/get-to-know-the-world-of-warcraft-forever-legacy-system); [Legacy challenge reference](https://senseos.gg/guides/wow-forever/legacy-system/))

| Category | Launch catalogue total | Milestones |
|---|---:|---|
| Classes | 27 | Each of nine classes at 25, 45, and 60 |
| Crafting professions | 18 | Each of six non-gathering primary professions at 150, 225, and 300 |
| PvP | 12 | Ranks 3/7/10/13/14; four battleground factions at Exalted; Field of Honor week-4/7/10 quests |
| Adventure | 2 | Complete-world exploration on one character; Lord Valthalak dungeon-set questline |
| Dungeons | 3 | Complete the final-boss checklists for the 15–25, 26–45, and 46–60 groups |
| Raids | 3 | Onyxia; all thirteen Hyjal Summit bosses; all eight Barrow Deeps bosses |

The eligible crafting set is Alchemy, Blacksmithing, Enchanting, Engineering, Leatherworking, and Tailoring. Herbalism, Mining, and Skinning supply materials and can be excellent economic choices; their skill milestones are not the crafting challenges in this table. Cooking, First Aid, and Fishing remain useful but likewise must not be counted as three extra point-producing crafting lines. ([Legacy challenge reference](https://senseos.gg/guides/wow-forever/legacy-system/))

Class challenges reward different **classes**, not endlessly repeated characters. A second Priest reaching 25 does not create a second “Priest 25” challenge. The equivalent principle applies to another character repeating an already completed crafting milestone. For account expansion, add a class or profession milestone the ledger is actually missing.

Dungeon challenges require a checklist, not one dungeon from each level range. Open the live challenge and read its remaining bosses before planning a trip. Forever adds dungeons, and community lists disagree around the boundaries of the bands. A route that simply substitutes the original Classic dungeon list can miss a required stop. We do not fabricate an exact completed checklist from a dungeon’s approximate recommended level.

The launch raid announcement schedules Barrow Deeps, Hyjal Summit, and Onyxia’s Lair for **9 December 2026**. Consequently the three raid points are future opportunities on this research date. A proposed first-week route that counts them is impossible under that schedule. ([Blizzard launch schedule](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-gb/news/24315820/world-of-warcraft-forever-goes-live-globally-4-november))



## The account arithmetic before choosing a route

Keep a ledger of unique completed challenges rather than mentally counting characters. Let **C** be unique class milestones, **P** unique eligible crafting milestones, **D** dungeon checklist completions, **A** adventure challenges, **V** PvP challenges, and **R** raid challenges. Then earned points are `E = C + P + D + A + V + R`, while a non-Hardcore character’s available allocation is `min(E, 16)` under launch rules.

This simple accounting catches several common errors. A level-60 Priest has completed three class milestones, not sixty. Two eligible professions at 300 contribute six milestones, not two. Two characters with the same profession at the same thresholds do not duplicate those challenges. Completing a raid that contains several bosses earns the particular raid challenge once, not a Legacy point per boss.

An illustrative single-character route with two fixed crafting professions can supply three class points plus six profession points, for **nine**. Add all three dungeon checklists and both adventure achievements and the total becomes **fourteen**. Reaching sixteen then requires at least two eligible PvP or raid challenges, or broadening the plan to new class/profession milestones elsewhere. This is arithmetic about those selected categories, not a claim that fourteen is the maximum obtainable by a character who changes professions repeatedly.

If the Priest keeps one gathering profession and one crafting profession, its class-plus-current-professions subtotal is six rather than nine. With two gathering professions it is three. That does **not** prove gathering is bad. Gathering can finance the new crafting milestones on another character and reduce the time the whole account spends short of gold. Count net development across the account rather than comparing only the number printed beside the main.

The phrase “account-optimal” needs an objective. Fastest Priest to 60, earliest sixteen Legacy points, best long-term profession coverage, strongest dungeon healer next weekend, and least total gold expenditure are different problems. Without local material prices, hours available, leveling proficiency, and group access, nobody can honestly prove one route wins all of them. Our default objective is useful progress with little waste: make the Priest playable now, obtain inexpensive unique milestones along the way, and invest in alts when their benefits justify the interruption.



## Recommended first allocation: the solo-first Priest

For a new Priest who spends most active time questing alone, allocate the **first five earned Legacy points into Thrill of Adventure**, one at a time. This is a recommendation based on the perk’s fit to that activity, not an official class ranking. The perk returns one percent of maximum health and mana per rank over ten seconds after a qualifying killing blow; it does not work in dungeons, raids, or battlegrounds. ([Thrill reference](https://www.wowhead.com/news/how-do-legacy-points-work-in-wow-forever-383029))

The important word is **earned**. Point one may arrive at a crafting threshold or level 25. Point two might arrive much later. The recommendation should be useful throughout that interval, not only after the complete build is assembled. A direct benefit from one point is valuable when the next unlock is uncertain.

Thrill helps a solo Priest in two ways: recovered mana reduces future drink requirements, and recovered health can reduce the need for a heal that would itself consume mana. The second effect is situational; it is not correct to convert every recovered health point into a fixed amount of saved mana. If you were already healthy or were about to receive an efficient heal, some recovery is wasted. Evaluate the perk by how often it changes your next action.

For an illustrative Priest with a 1,200-mana maximum, rank one’s nominal complete return is twelve mana per eligible kill and rank five’s is sixty. Ten fully realized rank-five triggers would total six hundred mana before waste or interaction with overlapping triggers. This is an accounting example, not a promise that rapid kills stack ten independent recovery effects. The retrieved descriptions do not establish the exact overlap behavior, so this guide does not multiply rapid-kill throughput into a guaranteed regeneration rate.

The perk rewards qualifying killing blows, not merely healing someone who kills the enemy. A Priest who spends nearly all playtime healing dungeon groups should not copy this allocation blindly. A dedicated duo healer who rarely lands the final hit also receives less value than a solo quester. You can share an account total while giving that healing character a different allocation.

After the first five points, decide again rather than following a rigid script. If the Priest still has a substantial leveling journey ahead and you are developing alts, **Well Rested 5/5 followed by Talented** is a reasonable long-term continuation. If the Priest is already nearing 60 when those later points arrive, an economy or recovery perk may be more useful than unlocking a leveling-only advantage just before it expires.

| Earned points on a solo-first account | Suggested allocation on the leveling Priest | Decision it supports |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | No Legacy allocation | Play the actual fresh-start talent route |
| 1–5 | Thrill of Adventure 1/5 through 5/5 | Immediate open-world sustain |
| 6–10 | Add Well Rested toward 5/5 | Prepare for useful rested play and the Talented prerequisite |
| 11–15 | Add Talented toward 5/5, if substantial leveling remains | Reach later talents earlier |
| 16 | One useful first-row flex perk, or reconsider the whole layout | Fit the next stage of play |

This order deliberately postpones Talented relative to an established alt build. You began with no points, and leveling sustain can help you earn the next points. It is not rational to describe an unowned ten-point combination as the automatic first purchase. If you earn six to ten points unusually early through broad crafting or alt progress, use the next section’s alternative instead.



## Talented: powerful earlier access, no level-60 bonus points

Talented has five ranks and requires **Well Rested 5/5**. Its first rank therefore requires six total points committed to that path; all five ranks require ten. It advances the level at which class-talent acquisition starts by one level per rank, down to level 5. The final class-talent cap remains **51**. ([Legacy prerequisite reference](https://wowforevercompass.com/legacy/))

For character level `L` and Talented rank `r`, with `r` between zero and five, the useful planning formula is:

`classTalentPoints(L, r) = min(51, max(0, L - 9 + r))`

At level 20 a zero-Legacy character has eleven talent points; rank-five Talented makes that sixteen. At level 40 the comparison is thirty-one versus thirty-six. At level 55 both routes have forty-six versus fifty-one, and at level 60 both have fifty-one. If a calculator offers fifty-six at level 60 merely because you bought Talented, that calculator is applying the wrong model.

| Level | No Talented | Talented 1/5 | Talented 5/5 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 0 | 0 | 1 |
| 9 | 0 | 1 | 5 |
| 10 | 1 | 2 | 6 |
| 20 | 11 | 12 | 16 |
| 25 | 16 | 17 | 21 |
| 35 | 26 | 27 | 31 |
| 40 | 31 | 32 | 36 |
| 50 | 41 | 42 | 46 |
| 55 | 46 | 47 | 51 |
| 60 | 51 | 51 | 51 |

For a milestone requiring a total of `m` points committed to one tree, and `o` earlier points spent outside it, the earliest theoretical level is `m + o + 9 - r`. It is theoretical because the build must still satisfy row and prerequisite requirements. An eleven-point milestone is level 20 with no diversion and no Talented. Spending two earlier points in another tree moves it to 22. Rank-five Talented moves those examples to 15 and 17 respectively.

This explains why two useful points in Wand Specialization have a real timing cost: the points improve the route but delay the next deep-tree unlock. Neither taking nor skipping the wand points is universally wrong. Compare the value of the wand during the intervening levels with the value of the earlier milestone. The detailed class builds should say explicitly which tradeoff they choose.

Talented also does not bypass the training level of a baseline spell. Receiving a talent point early and being allowed to train a particular ranked spell early are different rules. Do not infer that every ability associated with a specialization moves five levels earlier. When purchasing Talented during an existing character’s journey, inspect the talent window and reconcile its available total before continuing a level-by-level plan.

For an established account, a legal general-purpose leveling allocation is **Well Rested 5 + Talented 5 + Thrill 5 + one first-row flex point = 16**. Well Rested and Talented occupy ten points; sustain takes five. The last point can support an actual side activity. It is not a claim that a profession perk must be bought just because one slot remains.



## Well Rested and travel perks: what their percentages mean

Well Rested increases rested accumulation speed and the rested cap by four percent per rank, reaching twenty percent for each at full rank. It is not twenty percent more experience from every quest, enemy, dungeon, and profession action. Its value depends on how much rest the character accumulates and then consumes. ([Adventure client data](https://wow-forever.gg/legacy/adventure/))

A regularly played main that burns through rest early in a long session and an alt played for an hour every few days have different rested profiles. The alt may receive much more practical value from the same allocation. This is another reason account-wide **points** should not mean account-wide **identical choices**.

If a rest pool is already capped, faster accumulation produces nothing until capacity opens. A higher cap can matter during a long absence, but only if the character later uses that stored rest. Conversely, a character constantly consuming every available rested point can benefit from accumulation even when it never reaches the cap. Separate those two effects when estimating the perk’s usefulness.

The attractive travel perk is Frequent Flier: fifty percent cheaper flight paths and a flight mount that travels twenty percent faster. It requires ten points already in Adventure and **Field Guide 3/3**. A suggested build with only Field Guide 1/3 fails that prerequisite. Field Guide’s three ranks reduce the camp-feature placement cooldown by eight, seventeen, and twenty-five percent. ([Flight prerequisite reference](https://www.stillsixty.com/legacy))

A legal sixteen-point travel-oriented leveling allocation is **Well Rested 5 + Talented 5 + Field Guide 3 + Frequent Flier 1 + Thrill 2**. This gives up three ranks of open-world recovery relative to the sustain build. Choose it when the character actually takes enough flights and uses enough camps to justify that exchange.

Twenty percent faster flight speed does not mathematically mean twenty percent less flight time. On an unchanged path with no fixed delays, time becomes `oldTime / 1.2`, approximately 83.3 percent of the original. A nominal twelve-minute flight becomes ten minutes, saving two. Loading, takeoff, landing, route shape, and other fixed components can reduce the realized saving. The gold discount is simpler: a normally priced forty-silver trip becomes twenty silver before any other independently applicable pricing effects.

Field Guide alone is also not a movement bonus. Buying it as the prerequisite means spending points on a camping convenience to reach a flight convenience. Compare the whole package with the perks it displaces. “The final perk costs only one point” is misleading when its required path consumes several more.



## Different sixteen-point jobs for different characters

Do not leave leveling perks untouched forever out of habit. At 60, Talented cannot add to the fifty-one class points, and a rested-experience perk no longer advances that capped character’s level. Reconsider them when the character’s principal activity changes. Read the trainer’s actual reset price before paying; the live cost is not securely established by the sources retrieved for this chapter.

A battleground-focused example is **Gourmand 3, The Quick and the Dead 2, For Great Honor 5, Diplomat 5, Reagent Economy 1**. The first five points open the next row; ten points are present before taking Reagent Economy, and its required Quick and the Dead ranks are complete. This is a legal progression-oriented allocation for Honor, reputation, food-buff convenience, and rebuffing. It does not claim to grant sixteen points of direct PvP damage or healing. ([Resourcefulness client data](https://wow-forever.gg/legacy/resourcefulness/))

The Honor and reputation bonuses each reach ten percent at five ranks in the current tree data. For an eligible gain of `x`, a simple isolated ten-percent modifier produces `1.10x` before the game’s rounding and any interactions with other bonuses. It is not evidence that ranking takes exactly ten percent less calendar time: caps, weekly timing, queues, objectives, and losses can be more important than the gain multiplier.

An example for a character developing professions and selling gathered materials is **Working Overtime 5, Bountiful Harvest 5, Bartering 2, Dedicated Study 1, Performance Bonus 3**. Its total is sixteen. Bountiful Harvest is complete before Performance Bonus, and Bartering is complete with more than ten points invested before Dedicated Study. ([Professions client data](https://wow-forever.gg/legacy/professions/))

Choose this only when its activities match the character. Bountiful Harvest affects **Scarce** gathering materials; it is not a promise to double every ordinary herb, ore, or skin. A character with two crafting professions and no gathering has little reason to buy that path simply because an economy template looks impressive. Similarly, Working Overtime improves skill-up chance; it is not automatic bonus items from every craft.

Dedicated Study increases the lowest primary or secondary tradeskill. A neglected secondary profession can therefore receive the increase instead of the expensive primary profession you hoped to advance. The alternative Essence reward requires both current primary professions and all three secondary professions at 300. Planning around free Essences without meeting those conditions is premature. A daily convenience is useful, but it cannot replace reading its target-selection rule.

Bartering discounts vendor goods. Development notes removed its training and repair discounts; do not budget your mount training, class training, or repairs as though they automatically receive the vendor-item reduction. If a purchase is important enough to shape your route, verify the quoted price in its own window. ([Blizzard development notes](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/wow-forever-beta-development-notes-%E2%80%93-updated-november-2/2360696/1))

For a healer who keeps two crafting professions and actively cooks and fishes, a different legal economy example is **Working Overtime 5, Bartering 2, Master Chef 5, Dedicated Study 1, Luremaster 2, and The Quick and the Dead 1**. Buy Working Overtime first, then Bartering and three ranks of Master Chef to reach ten Professions points before Dedicated Study. Finish the cooking and fishing ranks afterward. This avoids buying gathering perks on a character that never gathers, but it remains conditional on actually using Cooking and Fishing; it is not a healing-throughput build.

A dungeon healer can also value recovery and consumable convenience, but its best full allocation depends on how much it quests, crafts, raids, and PvPs outside dungeons. Do not spend five points in Thrill solely for dungeon mana recovery: its exclusion is explicit. Avoid offering a fabricated universal “Holy Legacy BiS” when the perks mostly optimize activities around the encounter.



## Three realistic paths from zero toward sixteen

### Route A: play the Priest first

Level the Priest, maintain an economically sustainable profession plan, and complete dungeon checklists as opportunities appear. Buy immediate solo sustain with early Legacy points. If you enjoy PvP, count only the precise rank, reputation, or season challenges you actually finish. This is the best default when the user’s main wish is to play a Human Priest rather than operate a production schedule.

Its advantage is focus. Gold, training attention, bags, and player familiarity accumulate on the character that performs the requested role. Its limitation is that the earliest six to ten Legacy points may arrive late enough that the main receives less value from Talented. That is acceptable: the same account points remain available to future alts, and the main can allocate toward its current work.

### Route B: broaden the account without repeating milestones

A concrete, auditable sixteen-point example is a Priest at 60 with two eligible crafting professions at 300, worth nine points; an alt of a different class at 25 with two previously uncompleted professions at 150, worth three more; and a second different-class alt at 25 with the final two eligible professions at 150, worth three more. That totals **fifteen**. Raising one of those alt professions to 225 supplies the sixteenth unique milestone.

For example, the professions could be Tailoring/Enchanting on the Priest, Alchemy/Engineering on alt A, and Blacksmithing/Leatherworking on alt B. This distribution is an illustration of distinct challenge coverage, **not** the recommended low-capital profession pairing. It has no gatherer and can be expensive. Use it to audit arithmetic, then replace the economic plan with one that fits material supply. A ninth profession somehow yielding another point is not hiding in the subtotal.

The same target can be reached through many combinations: an uncompleted dungeon band, a genuinely achievable PvP challenge, an additional class milestone, or another crafting threshold may be a better final point. Compare the remaining work from the account’s present state rather than restarting the whole plan around a theoretical optimum.

### Route C: alternate characters on rested schedules

This approach fits limited daily sessions and an interest in several classes. Play the Priest until its next useful stopping point, leave it in an appropriate resting location, then play an alt whose rested pool and uncompleted milestones offer efficient progress. Give each character its own Legacy allocation. The Priest can keep sustain while an alt that already has ten available points starts with the Well Rested/Talented combination.

The benefit is not magic free progress: time spent leveling an alt is time not spent leveling the Priest. It pays off when the new class/profession milestones, rested usage, material production, and enjoyment exceed that opportunity cost. A player who dislikes the alt may abandon the plan before its supposed efficiency materializes. A sustainable route usually beats an elaborate route that nobody wants to complete.

Before choosing the next milestone, write down five quantities: the missing challenge, estimated playtime, gold/material cost, whether the activity produces useful gear or stock, and which character benefits from the resulting point. Use estimates rather than pretending launch material prices are stable. Then revisit the estimate after the activity. This produces an account plan based on your own evidence.
